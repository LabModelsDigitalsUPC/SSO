Introduction back to ToC

Structural analysis results produced by finite-element tools are typically locked into proprietary, tool-specific file formats and disconnected from the BIM/knowledge-graph representations of the same building or infrastructure asset. O-SAM is a tool-agnostic JSON interchange format for structural analysis models developed to bridge this gap; SSO is its RDF/OWL counterpart, letting O-SAM models be published, queried, and linked as first-class nodes in a knowledge graph. A companion converter (`OSAMtoSSO.py`, in this ontology's repository) translates an O-SAM JSON export into an SSO RDF graph. The ontology is organized around five groups of classes: (1) the **structural model and its mesh** — `StructuralAnalysisModel`, `Object`, `Mesh`, `Node`, `Element` and its `Solid`/`Shell`/`Beam` subclasses; (2) **materials and sections** — `Material`, `ElasticBehaviour`/`PlasticBehaviour`, `Section` and its `Beam`/`Shell`/`Solid` subclasses, and the nine parametric `CrossSectionProfile` shapes; (3) **assembly** — `Assembly`, `Instance`, `Nset`/`Elset`; (4) **actions** — `LoadCase`, `Load` (point, distributed, surface) and `BoundaryCondition`; and (5) **provenance** — `Calibration`, built on `prov:Activity`, for tracking how a parameter's value came to be. See Section 1.1 for the namespaces SSO reuses or links out to.
